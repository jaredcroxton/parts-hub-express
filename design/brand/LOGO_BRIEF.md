## 1. The current logo

![Current logo from the old Shopify store](current-logo-old-store.jpg)

Taken from the old Shopify store header (`partshubexpresscom.com`). It is the only version we have.

What holds it back:

1. **It only exists as a tiny image.** A 150x73 pixel JPEG with a white box behind it. It blurs on anything bigger than a thumbnail and cannot go on signage, a ute door, a hi-vis shirt, an invoice or the new share tile.
2. **Too many parts.** A gear, an arrow swoosh, speed lines, a two-colour wordmark and a web address. At phone size it turns into a smudge.
3. **No symbol that stands alone.** The gear sits behind the letters, so there is nothing clean for the browser tab icon, the iPhone home screen icon or a Facebook profile picture.
4. **Clip-art signals.** A gear with a swoosh and speed lines is the stock logistics and auto parts template. It says generic parts, not crushing, screening and conveyors, and it looks like hundreds of other logos.
5. **Colours fight the new site.** Charcoal, steel blue and mustard against the site's black, white and Markout Orange. Blue with orange-yellow is also the category cliché we deliberately avoided.
6. **The web address is inside the logo.** It dates the logo, cannot be read small, and "Partshubexpress.com" does not match the store's actual address (`partshubexpresscom.com`).
7. **The name is spelt two ways.** "PARTSHUB EXPRESS" in the logo, "Parts Hub Express" on the new site. Pick one and use it everywhere.
8. **Unbalanced stack.** "EXPRESS" is smaller and pushed right under "PARTSHUB", so the lockup leans.

What is worth keeping: the name, the heavy confident letters, and the idea of speed. Say "fast" once, cleanly, instead of three times.

## 2. The brief

**Business.** Parts Hub Express, the online parts store of Australian Crushing and Belting Group (spelling to confirm with ACBG). Crusher, screen and conveyor wear parts, sold to maintenance planners, quarry and plant managers, crushing contractors and purchasing officers across Australia.

**The one idea.** The right part, found fast.

**Personality.** Engineered, direct, dependable. More like a workshop drawing than a truck-stop sign. No hype.

**Must have:**

- A **symbol** that works alone at 16 pixels (browser tab) and at 180 pixels (iPhone home screen, Facebook profile picture).
- A **wordmark** in heavy, clear letters that reads at a glance on a phone.
- **Lockups:** horizontal (symbol left, wordmark right), stacked, symbol only, wordmark only.
- **Two colours at most:** black `#000000` and Markout Orange `#F45120`, on white or black.
- **One-colour versions:** all black and all white, for embroidery on hi-vis, laser etching on steel, stamped part tags and fax-quality invoices.
- **Vector masters** (SVG and PDF), plus PNG exports.

**Must not have:** gears, swooshes, speed lines, gradients, shadows, 3D, chrome, a web address, a tagline inside the logo, or crusher silhouettes that resemble manufacturer marks (Metso, Sandvik, Terex Finlay, McCloskey, Kleemann, Extec). ACBG is an independent supplier, not an authorised dealer, and the logo must never suggest otherwise.

**Tie-ins to the new site** (useful, not compulsory):

- The small **numbered callout square** from the "Where each part fits" drawings.
- **Section-drawing line work:** crisp strokes, square corners, hatching.
- **Part numbers treated as type.**
- The **orange full stop** used in the interim "P." site icon.

**Deliverables:** SVG, PDF and PNG sets for every lockup; favicon (16, 32, 48); app icon (180, 512); social profile picture (400x400); email signature size; one-page brand sheet showing colours, clear space and minimum size.

## 3. Concept directions and prompts

### Which tool for what

- **Recraft (V3 or newer, Vector style):** the best start, because it outputs real vector SVG you can edit.
- **Nano Banana Pro or GPT Image (in Higgsfield):** fast concept boards and mockups. Raster only.
- **Midjourney (v7):** loose exploration. Raster only.

Two rules that save hours:

1. **Generate symbols without words.** A single letter such as P is fine; whole words are not, because AI image models still misspell them. Set the wordmark in real type afterwards, in vector (Inter Tight ExtraBold matches the site; a licensed industrial grotesk is the alternative).
2. **Treat every result as a sketch.** A designer redraws the chosen idea in vector. That gives clean curves, and it gives ACBG a logo it can own (see section 4).

### Base prompt (paste first, then add one direction below)

> Minimal flat vector logo symbol for Parts Hub Express, an Australian online store for crusher, screen and conveyor wear parts. The idea is "the right part, found fast". Bold geometric shapes, thick uniform strokes, square corners, engineered and precise. Black #000000 with a single small accent in orange #F45120, on a plain white background. Solid filled shapes, three shapes at most, no outlines inside outlines, no inner lines, no maze patterns, every gap at least as wide as the stroke. Must stay recognisable at 16 pixels. Must not look like an upload or share icon, a play button, a medical cross or a road-sign arrow. No words (a single letter is fine when the concept asks for one), no gradients, no shadows, no 3D, no gears, no swooshes, no speed lines, no mockup. Centred, generous white space.

For Midjourney, add: `--style raw --v 7 --ar 1:1 --no words, maze, inner lines, gradient, shadow, 3d, gear, swoosh, speed lines, mockup, photo`

**Round 1 lesson (15 September 2026).** The first version of this base prompt said "no text" while Direction A asked for a capital P. The tool avoided real letters and drew maze-like pseudo-letters instead. Four results came back: a chevron tag (too many strokes, smudges at 16 pixels), a bracket plus arrowhead (best silhouette, but the brackets are noise and a plain arrow is generic), an arrow out of a box (reads as the iPhone share icon) and a maze cross (reads as a medical cross). The base prompt above is fixed. Carry forward only the bold arrowhead with the orange tip.

### Direction A: The callout (recommended first)

A heavy square outline, like the numbered callout markers on engineering drawings, holding a bold capital P, with a small solid orange square in the lower right corner acting as a full stop. It says "this is the part" and extends the interim site icon.

> [Base prompt] Concept: a thick black square outline, like a callout marker on an engineering drawing, containing a heavy geometric capital P. A small solid orange square sits at the lower right like a full stop. Strict grid, square corners, balanced negative space.

### Direction B: The jaw V

Two heavy bars converging into a V, the shape of a jaw crusher's plates, which also reads as a check mark ("found it") and a forward arrowhead. One bar orange.

> [Base prompt] Concept: two thick straight bars converging into a sharp V, echoing the jaw plates of a crusher seen in cross-section. The V also reads as a check mark and a downward arrowhead. Left bar black, right bar orange. A clear gap, as wide as a bar, where they nearly meet.

### Direction C: The hub nut

A hexagon (a hex nut, and a "hub") with a forward-facing notch or arrow cut into one side. Hub, parts and express in one shape.

> [Base prompt] Concept: a solid black hexagon like a heavy hex nut, with a crisp arrow-shaped notch cut into its right side to suggest forward movement. Optional small orange square in the centre hole. Precise angles, no bevels.

### Direction D: The part tag

The wordmark itself, set like a stamped steel part tag: heavy rectangular border, a punched hole on the left, letters in tabular engineering type. Works as a badge on invoices, packaging and the website header.

> [Base prompt, but allow text] Concept: a rectangular stamped steel part tag with a heavy black border and a round punched hole on the left edge. Inside, bold condensed uppercase letters reading PARTS HUB EXPRESS, with a thin orange rule under the words. Flat, graphic, no metal texture.

(Expect spelling errors. Keep the layout idea and reset the letters in real type.)

### Direction E: Find the part

A drawing registration crosshair (the target marks on technical drawings) with a bold P at its centre. Says "find the right part" and fits the site's drawing language.

> [Base prompt] Concept: a thin-stroked crosshair target mark from a technical drawing, with a heavy geometric capital P centred in it. The crosshair lines stop short of the P. One small orange square marks the top right quadrant.

### Concept board prompt (Nano Banana Pro or GPT Image)

> Logo exploration sheet on a clean white background: a 3x3 grid of nine different minimal flat vector logo symbols for "Parts Hub Express", an Australian online store for crusher, screen and conveyor wear parts. Black #000000 with small accents of orange #F45120. Explore: a callout square with a bold P and an orange full stop; two converging bars forming a V like crusher jaw plates; a hex nut with an arrow notch; a stamped part tag; a drawing crosshair with a P. Thick uniform strokes, square corners, no gradients, no shadows, no 3D, no gears, no swooshes. Each symbol small and centred in its cell with a thin grey label area left blank.

### Wordmark exploration (then rebuild in real type)

> Wordmark reading "PARTS HUB EXPRESS" in a heavy, tight, modern grotesk, uppercase, black on white, one line. The letter spacing is slightly tightened and even. A small solid orange square follows the final S like a full stop. Flat vector, no effects, no symbol, no tagline.

### Mockup prompts for showing ACBG (after a direction is chosen)

> Photorealistic mockup of a high-visibility orange work shirt with a small embroidered one-colour black logo on the chest [describe the chosen symbol], natural light, workshop background out of focus.

> Photorealistic mockup of a white utility vehicle door with a large black and orange vinyl decal of [the chosen horizontal logo lockup], Australian quarry background, overcast light.

> Close-up photo of a stamped stainless steel part tag on a crusher wear part, laser-etched with [the chosen symbol] and a part number, shallow depth of field.

(Mockups are for presentation only. Never use generated images of parts on the website.)

## 4. Picking a winner, and making it ACBG's

**Test every shortlisted symbol before choosing:**

1. **16 pixel test:** shrink it to browser-tab size. Can you still tell what it is?
2. **One-colour test:** does it work all black and all white?
3. **Squint test:** does it read from across the yard?
4. **Hi-vis test:** does it survive embroidery (no thin lines, no tiny gaps)?
5. **Neighbour test:** next to manufacturer logos, does it look independent, with no borrowed shapes?
6. **Share tile test:** does it sit well on the black share image?

**Making it ownable:**

- Have a designer redraw the chosen concept by hand in vector. In Australia, copyright protects work with human authorship, so a purely AI-generated image may not be protected, while a designer's redrawn version is on much firmer ground.
- Before committing, search the Australian Trade Mark Search on IP Australia for "Parts Hub", "PartsHub" and similar names, in class 7 (machine parts) and class 35 (online retail), and check the symbol visually against manufacturer marks. Consider registering the final mark. A trade mark attorney can confirm.

**When the logo is approved,** we swap it into the site header and footer, replace the interim "P." icon (favicon, iPhone icon), and regenerate the share tile (design/brand/share-tile).
